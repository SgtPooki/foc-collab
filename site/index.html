Games with no server

Every game here runs in your browser with Filecoin Onchain Cloud as the only backend. Moves are signed JSON pieces in data sets; boards are a deterministic fold over them. No server, no database, nothing to take down.

        tic-tac-toe bring your own wallet

        Two players, two wallets, two data sets. Connect a wallet on Filecoin calibration once (about three minutes and three or four signatures); after that a short-lived AddPieces-only session key signs your moves. Your opponent is found from chain events, so the page carries no key of anyone's. Anyone with an invite link can watch without a wallet. Moves settle in about a minute.

        connect four bring your own wallet

        Seven columns, six rows, four in a row wins. Same wallet, same session key, same rules underneath: your discs settle in your data set, your opponent's in theirs, and both pages fold both. Red is the creator, Yellow joins.

        chat no wallet needed

        A lobby room and a room per game. Players post into the data set their wallet pays for; anyone else posts as a guest into the arcade's data set, which a contract lets each guest write to once every couple of minutes, from a budget the arcade funds. Every reader folds every data set. About a minute per post.

        FOC Corgi community pet, bring your own wallet

        A dog whose life is the real funding runway of its own Filecoin storage. Feeding it is a USDFC deposit into its account, in your name, on-chain. Money keeps it alive; only distinct people keep it happy. Feed it enough and you adopt a corgi of your own in the park.

        How to play

        • Get a browser wallet with a calibration account holding a little tFIL and at least 5 USDFC: tFIL faucet, USDFC faucet.
        • Open a game, press "connect wallet to play", approve the prompts.
        • Create a game and share the link, or open a link you were sent and join.

        Under the hood

        • Your wallet pays for your own writes; the other player pays for theirs.
        • Ordering across data sets uses only the chain-assigned piece id inside each author's own data set plus signed hash links, so nobody can steal a seat or take a move back after the reply.
        • Discovery reads PieceAdded events from the chain; no lobby server, no publisher key.
